---
name: Media reference grounding
description: Scene IDs and whole-book identities need semantic and life-stage review before paid image generation.
---

Canonical IDs can be valid while pointing to the wrong story location or life stage. Validate the actual source-described setting and age before treating a saved scene plan as a complete visual brief. Keep necessary corrections in the production snapshot; do not silently rewrite the original Book Intelligence analysis.

**Why:** Frankenstein's Lake Como adoption scene was linked to a Lake Geneva / Belrive environment. Whole-book adult character records also caused a childhood reference to look adolescent, even after its age and main prompt were changed.

**How to apply:** Review geography and chronology as well as ID membership. For childhood variants, override adult clothing, body proportions, posture, role and later-life relationships—not just the age label. Preserve source-supported identity traits and clearly label unspecified casting details as production choices.

Neutral character-study backgrounds are intentional reference designs, not missing narrative environments. Keep reported quality scores and notes; record explicit review decisions rather than lowering the threshold merely to hide a false-positive flag.

**Why:** An early evaluator penalized canonical character portraits for not containing a scene-specific environment.

**How to apply:** Quality prompts must distinguish character references from finished scene compositions. Genuine age/location failures still require correction or explicit editorial rejection.
